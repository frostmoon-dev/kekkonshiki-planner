What is needed

The journey, step by step

The traditional adat steps, then the paperwork and bookings. Timings are a suggested plan, not a rule.

MerisikAn older relative of the groom visits the bride's family to ask if she is free to marry.
MeminangThe formal proposal. Families agree on wang hantaran, mas kahwin and dates.
BertunangEngagement. Ring and gift trays are exchanged.
BerinaiHenna night, a few days before the nikah.
Akad nikahThe marriage contract. Groom says the ijab kabul before the wali and witnesses.
BersandingThe reception. The couple sits on the pelamin for family and guests.

⚠️ Verify: course, HIV screening and nikah application rules and fees differ by state. Check your state religious department (JAIN) or the SPPIM portal before booking dates.

Where the money goes

What the research says • Intimate: about 50 close family. Nikah at the masjid, then lunch at home or a restaurant room. No pelamin, one outfit each. Lands near RM9,800 before wang hantaran. ⚠️ Ask your masjid about booking rules and fees. • Small wedding (Kecil): about 150 family and friends, home or community hall, mini pelamin. Lands near RM19,000 before wang hantaran. Most items below the published ranges are my estimates, because the guides price full-size weddings. • A 2026 guide puts a Malay wedding at about RM30,000–RM80,000. Catering is about 35–50% of the total. • A UMK professor puts Malaysian weddings overall at RM50,000–RM200,000. Sources disagree, and none state their method. • The biggest lever is guest count. Each 100 guests at RM45 per pax is RM4,500 of catering, plus door gifts.
Colour planning

Choose a palette

Tap one to make it your theme. The ribbons on this page change to match. Many couples wear a lighter tone for akad nikah and a stronger one for bersanding.

Rules that keep a palette working 1. Use 60 / 30 / 10. One main colour, one support colour, one metal or accent. 2. Match the groom's samping to the bride's accent, not her main colour. It looks planned, not copied. 3. Test fabric under the hall's real lighting. Warm hall lights turn grey and baby blue dull. 4. Give the pelamin your strongest colour. Photos are built around it. 5. Pick the palette early. Tell the mak andam, pelamin vendor and tailor the same hex codes.
Weight loss target and plan

Your weight plan

Enter your own numbers. The plan aims for a safe 0.5–1 kg per week, and stops losing 4 weeks before the wedding so your final baju fitting stays accurate.

Example numbers are filled in. Replace them with yours.

BMI uses Malaysia's Asian cut-offs: overweight from 23, obese from 27.5. Calories use the Mifflin-St Jeor formula. These are estimates. A doctor or dietitian should check them, especially if you have a health condition.

Phase 1 · first 4 weeks

Build the habits

  • Weigh once a week, same day, morning, after the toilet.
  • Use the Suku Suku Separuh plate: ¼ rice, ¼ protein, ½ vegetables and fruit.
  • Walk 7,000+ steps a day. Add more later.
  • Cut sweet drinks first. This is the easiest big win.
Phase 2 · main stretch

Lose steadily

  • Eat near the daily calorie number above.
  • Strength training 2–3 times a week. It keeps muscle, so the baju fits better.
  • Protein at every meal: fish, chicken, eggs, tempeh, tofu.
  • Plan for kenduri and Raya. Eat a normal meal, then go back to plan the next day.
Phase 3 · last 4 weeks

Hold and fit

  • Stop the deficit. Eat at maintenance.
  • Final baju nikah and sanding fitting at the start of this phase.
  • No crash diets or detox teas. They cause tiredness and puffiness on the day.
  • Sleep 7–8 hours. Drink water. Keep salt normal.
